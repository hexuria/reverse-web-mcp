the structural claim

Model calls do not grow with the work

Same app, same tasks, same harness, same commit. Only the arm changes. A loop stops to think once per dependency level and again per batch; a compiled plan thinks once and the scheduler climbs the rest.

the replication

It is not one model's habit

If only one model were tested, the obvious objection is that the result is a quirk of its habits. Swapping the model and changing nothing else leaves the shape intact: ours takes one call on both, the loop takes four to fourteen on both.

the clock

The thinking is the cost, not the doing

Execution runs in tens of milliseconds, or under a second when it is waiting on a webhook. The single planning call is nearly all of the wall time, which is exactly why a plan cache matters: a repeat goal costs zero calls.

what to believe

Reading it honestly

  • The difference that holds is calls, not width. Parallel tool calls parallelise fine: the loop went 50 wide on the 300-row task. What it cannot do is stop thinking between levels.
  • Correctness is close when the harness is fair. The loop is right on almost everything at depth 3 to 6. It falls over on scale: on 300 rows it needs fourteen calls and three minutes, and the browser-driven variant never finished.
  • The guarantees are structural, not prompted. Idempotency keys, spend gates, event waits and the join order are inserted by the compiler, so no amount of model drift can skip them. Zero double-sends across every arm and task is the visible consequence.
  • Five runs is a smoke signal, not a proof. Two models, twelve tasks, five runs per cell. Enough to see a shape; not enough to publish a confidence interval.
Four things this benchmark caught us doing wrong. A stale binary that made a smoke test measure old code. A silent fallback to a hand-written plan when the planner failed, scoring a model failure as a success. An app bug that erased payments only for the slower arms, which made the loops look worse than they were and forced a retraction of an earlier claim. And a quantifier whose meaning depended on position, so "one report over ten invoices" compiled into ten reports. Each one is fixed, pinned by a test, and disclosed here rather than quietly rerun.
The pixel arm is not in this table. A screenshot-and-click loop was built and run: 40 clicks per task, about 760k tokens, nothing finished, and no prompt-tuning parity with the other arms. It is the straw the whole design exists to avoid, and it is reported as the straw rather than as a scalp.
Provenance and how to reproduce

Rust workspace, roughly fifty commits, every one green through make gate: rustfmt, clippy with warnings denied, and the full test suite including in-process end-to-end runs. The target app is ours, with five doors and an oracle that counts effects independently of any arm's claim. Cells come from results/campaign-5-final (ours and the script, eleven tasks, five runs), campaign-5-loops (the loops, same commit, three runs) and campaign-4-luna (the second model). Reproduce with make bench-ocx ARMS=D,E,B2,C PLANNER=model RUNS=5, then make verify.
